# Prompted Strategic Personas in LLMs: A Chess-Based Study

## Overview
This project investigates whether **persona prompting can influence the long-horizon decision-making behavior of Large Language Models (LLMs)**. 

The study uses **chess as a structured decision environment** where an LLM is prompted to adopt different strategic personas and choose moves based on the current board state.

Each move generated by the LLM is evaluated using a chess engine to measure decision quality.

---

## Research Question
Can strategic personas imposed through prompting meaningfully and consistently shape an LLM's decision-making behavior over long horizons?

---

## Method

The system follows this pipeline:

1. Generate the current **board state in FEN notation**
2. Provide the **list of legal moves**
3. Prompt the LLM with a **specific persona**
4. Extract the move from the LLM output
5. Validate and apply the move
6. Evaluate the move using a **Negamax search engine**
7. Log the results

Moves are categorized as:
- Good Move
- Mistake
- Blunder

---

## Personas Implemented

The following strategic personas were tested:

- **Clinical Attacker (Tal-style)** – aggressive attacking play  
- **Defensive Turtle (Petrosian-style)** – solid defensive strategy  
- **Pure Materialist** – prioritizes material advantage  
- **Center Controller (Morphy-style)** – focuses on development and center control  

---

## Key Observations

- Providing the **legal move list significantly reduces illegal outputs**
- Using **FEN board representation improves model understanding**
- Persona prompts appear to influence **early game decisions**
- Strategic consistency often degrades during **long games**
- The model sometimes repeats moves, requiring **threefold repetition detection**

---

## Future Work

Future experiments will include:

- establishing a **neutral baseline without persona prompts**
- generating **larger datasets of games**
- analyzing additional metrics such as center control and aggression
